# What the model comparison is trying to establish

The goal is to identify a useful, reproducible model for five-label classification of rendered PTB-XL ECG images, then establish how well that finding transfers to independent ECG images. There is no universal best ECG model that can be established from one dataset and one training run.

**Completed evidence as of 26 September 2026:** the fifth experiment, `ptbxl_spatiotemporal_image_v1_seed42`, is complete and verified (25 September, 23:17 UTC). It ran 20 epochs and restored validation-selected epoch 16. The spatiotemporal model is the **provisional stronger overall PTB-XL candidate**: validation macro AUROC is 0.8889 versus 0.8816; test macro AUROC is **0.8803 versus 0.8698**, with paired gain **0.0105** (95% patient-bootstrap interval **0.0045–0.0167**). Test macro F1 rises from **0.6131 to 0.6409**. See the [protocol](</Users/miriamistanboli/Downloads/ecg swiss/docs/ptbxl_spatiotemporal_protocol.md>), [report](</Users/miriamistanboli/Downloads/ecg swiss/docs/ptbxl_spatiotemporal_report.md>) and [paired comparison](</Users/miriamistanboli/Downloads/ecg swiss/results/comparisons/ptbxl_spatiotemporal_image_v1_seed42/comparison.md>).

The improvement is not uniform. Test MI recall rises from **43.27% to 56.18%**, but HYP recall falls from **32.44% to 27.86%**, leaving **189 of 262 HYP positives missed**; HYP recall also falls on validation. These findings motivate a future development study, not changes chosen from test outcomes.

1. **Make the comparison fair.** Keep patient partitions, input images, labels and evaluation rules fixed. The earlier Mendeley LDA/CNN results remain separate because their dataset and targets differ.
2. **Test a specific architectural question.** The completed spatiotemporal experiment replaces the LSTM with a temporal-convolution head over the same spatial CNN's image features. This gives evidence for a practical alternative for combining patterns across horizontal positions. Different head capacity and dropout placement mean it does not isolate one mechanism or establish that temporal convolution is generally superior. Its feature-head causality does not imply causal waveform or streaming inference.
3. **Compare the full error profile.** Use macro AUROC as the existing primary ranking measure, supported by precision–recall/F1 and every diagnosis's sensitivity. Report missed abnormalities and uncertainty as well as overall scores. Do not call individual label accuracy whole-ECG diagnostic accuracy.
4. **Check robustness before selecting a final research model.** Repeat promising configurations with several random seeds using development data; document stability, size and runtime. Further changes to thresholds, imbalance handling or preprocessing require separately frozen development protocols.
5. **Test transfer.** Freeze the chosen approach and assess independent data, including real scans/photographs if that is the intended application. The current clean rendering benchmark cannot establish that performance.

The PTB-XL test cohort had already been observed before the spatiotemporal experiment was specified. This comparison and additional same-test comparisons are exploratory, with no tuning on test predictions. Paired patient-bootstrap intervals describe sampling uncertainty conditional on these fitted models; they do not account for training seeds, adaptive model choices or external-domain variation. LSM and the remembered convolution term remain placeholders until their exact meanings are confirmed. A compact 23,881-parameter hybrid ViT began training on 1 November after the data audit and synthetic resource checks; its [frozen protocol](ptbxl_hybrid_vit_protocol.md) tests global feature attention. This is one candidate, with no assumption that it will win.

The immediate next-model deliverable is complete: an executed experiment, verified saved artifacts and a paired comparison against the saved PTB-XL CNN–LSTM. The fifth run records one final test inference; all **152 tests pass**, and **164 prior files and four prior log rows remain preserved**. Repeated-seed and independent-data studies are proposed next work, not completed results. A fresh full run requires a new frozen protocol/configuration and prior-log preservation baseline; changing the run ID alone is insufficient because the completed protocol locks the four-row log that preceded the now-completed fifth experiment.

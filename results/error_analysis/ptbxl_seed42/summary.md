## Page gain as an information channel

The renderer lowers the page-wide gain (10 → 5 → 2.5 … mm/mV) when an ECG's maximum amplitude would leave its row band, and the gain is visible through calibration-pulse height. High QRS voltage is itself a hypertrophy criterion, so gain carries label information. Training-set prevalence by gain:

| Gain (mm/mV) | Train ECGs | NORM | MI | STTC | CD | HYP |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | 1158 | 0.496 | 0.283 | 0.161 | 0.236 | 0.021 |
| 5 | 11552 | 0.502 | 0.247 | 0.214 | 0.202 | 0.063 |
| 2.5 | 4100 | 0.285 | 0.271 | 0.346 | 0.293 | 0.308 |
| <=1.25 | 274 | 0.215 | 0.292 | 0.405 | 0.358 | 0.380 |

AUROC of page gain alone (no image, no model):

| Partition | NORM | MI | STTC | CD | HYP |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| validation | 0.422 | 0.496 | 0.580 | 0.541 | 0.741 |
| test | 0.424 | 0.507 | 0.584 | 0.549 | 0.719 |

Reading: gain alone reaches HYP AUROC ≈0.72–0.74, and every model's HYP sensitivity is low (0.02–0.19 on validation) on pages at 5–10 mm/mV (the standard clinical print gain). HYP results on this benchmark therefore partly reflect amplitude conveyed by the rendering's gain choice and may not transfer to fixed-gain printouts.
